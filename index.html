ABOUT

What Is Fasting?

Not "not eating," but time to "reset"

The basics

Holding off on food for a set time

For a period you decide, you skip solid food and use it as a chance to rethink your eating habits.

What matters

The first 3 days and the recovery period

The first 3 days calm your appetite, and in the recovery period you slowly return to normal meals.

My approach

Safely, with accurate information

No hype about results, and I'll tell you the cautions too. Your health always comes first.

HISTORY

History of Fasting

A practice passed down around the world

Ancient times–

Fasting for prayer and discipline

A practice in many religions, such as Ramadan in Islam and Lent in Christianity. In Japan, too, it has long been part of Buddhist and Shugendo training.

20th century

Into medicine and daily life

In Germany it spread under medical supervision from the 1920s, and in Japan "fasting retreats" opened across the country in the Showa era.

Today

Research and new styles

In 2016, Yoshinori Ohsumi won the Nobel Prize for his research on autophagy. Easy-to-maintain methods like "16-hour fasting" are spreading.

Okinawa

The island of longevity and "hara hachi bu"

According to the Okinawa Centenarian Study, begun in the 1970s, elders of the past ate mainly vegetables, sweet potatoes, and tofu, stopping at "80% full," and are estimated to have lived on about 10–15% fewer calories than recommended.

As diets changed, Okinawa's ranking fell: men dropped to 26th in 2000 (the "26 Shock"), and by 2015 men ranked 36th and women 7th. The old way of eating may hold hints worth revisiting.

Sources: Okinawa Centenarian Study / Jiji Press: "Longevity prefecture Okinawa" a thing of the past

HOW TO

Method & Schedule

What matters most: "the first 3 days" and "the recovery period"

Before you start | A pharmacist's checklist

  • Talk to a doctor first if: you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a chronic condition or are under treatment, are still growing or elderly, or are underweight
  • If you take medication: drugs for blood sugar or blood pressure may work differently. Don't decide on your own — ask first
  • Stop immediately if you notice: severe dizziness, cold sweats, shaky hands, palpitations, headache, nausea, etc. See a doctor if it persists
  • Judge information carefully: be wary of claims like "guaranteed weight loss" and product pitches

Day 1 | Preparation

  • Evening-only fast (skip dinner)

Days 2–3 | Fasting phase (liquids only)

  • Vegetable juice + protein + probiotics
  • Drink plenty of fluids
  • Stop if you feel dizzy or very fatigued

Day 4 onward | Recovery phase (skip dinner only)

Continue until the end of the month.

  • For breakfast and lunch, start with easy-to-digest food like rice porridge and miso soup
  • Chew well and eat slowly
  • Sparkling water for hunger at night
  • Keep exercise light — stretching or a walk

Treat one month as one cycle, and repeat it for 3 months.

This is general information and not a substitute for diagnosis or treatment. If you have concerns, please consult a doctor or pharmacist.
Q&A

FAQ

A pharmacist answers the questions people have before starting

Won't I be too hungry to sleep at night?

You may feel hungry during the first few days, but hunger comes in waves and settles down after a while. Many people get through it with warm water, sparkling water, or kachu-yu.

On days when it's so hard you truly can't sleep, it's OK to eat a little something light. There's no need to sacrifice sleep to keep going.

Can I do it while working, doing housework, or raising kids?

The "12 hours overnight" is mostly while you sleep, so you can go about your day as usual.

We recommend planning liquid-only days for quieter days or days off. Avoid days with a lot of driving, heavy physical work, or intense exercise.

Can I do it while breastfeeding?

Research hasn't yet established whether fasting is safe while breastfeeding. Avoid liquid-only fasting, and start with "80% full" or "no late-night snacks."

If you're concerned about your milk supply or your baby's weight, stop right away and consult a doctor or midwife.

Can I do it if I take medication?

Blood-sugar-lowering drugs can cause low blood sugar, and blood pressure drugs can make you lightheaded. Medicines taken with meals may need their dose or timing adjusted.

Don't stop or reduce your medication on your own — consult your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

What if I get a headache or feel lightheaded?

It may be caused by dehydration or a sudden drop in caffeine from coffee or tea. First, make sure to drink plenty of fluids.

If you have severe dizziness, cold sweats, shaky hands, or palpitations, stop immediately and have something with sugar. See a doctor if symptoms continue.

Will I rebound after it's over?

Eating a lot all at once after fasting makes it easy for weight to come back. That's exactly why it's important to return to normal meals slowly during the "recovery period."

Keeping a weight log afterward helps you notice early when it starts creeping up. That's also why we share our logs with each other.

Should I exercise?

On liquid-only days, keep it to walks or light stretching. Once you're back to normal meals, gradually increase how much you move.

RESEARCH

Research Today

What we know, and what we don't

REPORT 01

Latest research findings

  • No major difference in weight loss between intermittent fasting and ordinary calorie restriction (review of 99 studies, 2025)
  • Simply restricting when you eat makes a difference of only about 1–2 kg (2025)
  • What matters is "how much you eat in a day." Choose a method you can stick with
  • Safety during breastfeeding is still unknown. Consult a doctor or midwife

Sources: BMJ 2025;389:e082007 / Front Nutr 2025 / Obesity 2025

REPORT 02

Autophagy: the cell's "cleanup crew"

  • A system that breaks down and recycles old parts of cells (2016 Nobel Prize)
  • That it becomes more active with hunger has been confirmed mainly in animal and cell experiments
  • In humans, one report found higher markers in people who restricted their eating window (2025, exploratory)
  • Another report found biological age about 2.5 years lower with a "fasting-mimicking diet" (2024, estimated values; used food from a company the researchers are involved with)
  • Claims like "it makes you younger" or "it stops aging" have not been proven

Sources: Nature Communications 2024 / J Physiol 2025

Drinking herbal tea while fasting
  • Many are caffeine-free, making them a good option for staying hydrated. Choose ones without added sugar
  • Drinking a lot of strong tea on an empty stomach can be hard on your stomach
  • While breastfeeding, the effects on breast milk are unknown for many herbs, so start small — and ask if you're worried
  • "Natural" doesn't always mean "safe." If you take medication, check for interactions with a pharmacist

BODY & PAIN

Does Losing Weight Ease Body Pain?

From research and my own experience

Knees

Lose 1 kg, and your knees carry about 4 kg less

In people with knee pain, each 1 kg lost reduced the load while walking by about 4 kg. Losing about 10% of body weight also eased the pain.

Lower back

More weight tends to mean more back pain

People with obesity tend to have higher rates of back pain. However, there's still little research on whether weight loss improves back pain.

Feet

Weight is one factor in heel pain

Body weight is one factor in plantar fasciitis. Combining weight loss with stretching and insoles is recommended.

Sources: Arthritis Rheum 2005 / JAMA 2013 (IDEA trial) / Am J Epidemiol 2010 / JOSPT 2023

My story | Plantar fasciitis (personal experience)

My heel pain wouldn't go away, and I was diagnosed with plantar fasciitis. Even shockwave therapy didn't relieve it. The turning point was losing weight and doing rehab. Little by little the pain eased, and I felt firsthand what the research says.

What I want to share as a pharmacist

  • Losing weight is something "you can do yourself" to reduce the strain on your knees, back, and feet
  • It's important to combine it with rehab and treatment
  • If pain persists, you feel numbness, or pain suddenly becomes severe, see an orthopedist

Okinawa today

Over 40% of men have obesity

In Okinawa, obesity (BMI 25+) affects 41.1% of men and 27.5% of women (FY2024). Among men in their 40s–50s it's over 50%, and both sexes exceed the national average.

Obesity & medical costs

More weight, higher medical costs

Compared with people of normal weight, medical costs are 9.8% higher at BMI 25–29.9 and 22.3% higher at BMI 30+ (Ohsaki Study). National medical expenditure is at a record 48 trillion yen.

Effects of weight loss

Start with 3%, aim for 5–10%

Losing 3% improves blood pressure, blood sugar, and lipids. Losing 5–10% brings even greater improvement.

What I want to share as a pharmacist

  • For someone weighing 60 kg, 3% is about 1.8 kg. Start with "2 kg first"
  • Preventing lifestyle diseases may mean fewer doctor visits and medications in the future
  • Each person's weight loss helps support healthcare costs in Okinawa and Japan (how big the effect is remains debated in research)
  • If you take medication, your dose may need adjusting. Consult your doctor or pharmacist
